# Mobile Standards

Best practices on approaching mobile application design, agnostic of device or platform.

## Tenets Of Mobility

- Accommodate the modality
- Respect the platform
- Champion the sensory design

## Accommodate The Modality

The Oxford Dictionary describes modality as “a particular mode in which something exists or is experienced or expressed.” The same application
can exist in many different places: on your watch, phone, car, fridge, etc. In terms of mobility, we should be concerned about the design for each
modality in which an application can exist.

### Nature Of The Interface

Be aware of all the ways a user can interact with an interface. A user is no longer limited to mouse clicks; she can scroll with her thumb, wave her
hand, raise a controller, and speak commands. Specific inputs and outputs must not be dismissed or overlooked by the designer. Preferred inputs
and outputs must be prioritized.

### The Physical Environment

Because technologies are no longer limited to a user’s desk, it is likely that the user will interact with a particular modality in a multitude of
environments. Is it sunny? Rainy? Cold? Hot? Dark? Humid? Crowded? Loud? Ensure any inputs are dynamic enough to be understood by the
application, and any outputs are dynamic enough to be understood by the user.

### Reason For Existing

Understand why a particular modality was developed and brought to market. This understanding helps delineate what aspects of an app to highlight
to the user. For example, playback controls for a music application might appear quite large on a smartwatch to allow for easy song controls, but will
appear smaller on a smartphone to accommodate extra content like album artwork. In either case, both of these devices are meant to be on-the-go,
which means a user wants to get in, select a song and be on her way.

## Respect The Platform

The platform provides the standard (in our case - software standard) on which an application will exist. For example, popular smartphone and tablet
platforms include iOS and Android.

### User Expectations

Users of a platform already have a sense of how the platform operates. An application’s design should not alienate the environment a user
subscribes to.

### Following Standards

Platforms have an established set of standards. Following platform standards provides our designs with guidelines for gestures, layout and
navigation.

### Change Over Time

Platforms come and go over time. Applications and their designs will have to be modified to follow the guidelines of any new specific platform.

## Champion The Sensory Design

When limited by the affordances of a particular modality, and obliged to follow platform guidelines, the remaining creative space is reserved for the
manipulation of the application’s design.

### Use All Of The Senses

Customize visual, aural, tactile (and perhaps in the future, olfactory and gustatory) aspects of the application.

### Create An Identity

Design elements give the designer flexibility in making an application distinct from its peers. This links the application’s identity across modalities
and platforms.

## Designing For Mobility

### Do

- Address all user inputs and device outputs.
- Validate the design in a variety of physical environments.
- Understand why a particular modality exists.
- Synchronize platform standards with designs.
- Recognize that platforms change over time.
- Champion the sensory design.
- Consider design beyond the visual senses.
- Design specific to the modality.

### Don't

- Assume the user is stationary while using the application.
- Assume modalities are static.
- Alienate a user's expectations of a platform.
- Equate mobility with smartphone-only designs.
