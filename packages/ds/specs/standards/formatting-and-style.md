# Formatting and Style

A deeper dive into style nuances for digital content.

## Date Formatting

When abbreviating dates to month/day/year, do not use a leading 0 if the leading digit is a single digit. Always write out the full four-digit year for all dates. Example: `2/1/2023`.

Abbreviate the month when screen real estate is not available. Be consistent within the same screen.

- January: Jan
- February: Feb
- March: Mar
- April: Apr
- May: May
- June: Jun
- July: Jul
- August: Aug
- September: Sept
- October: Oct
- November: Nov
- December: Dec

### Dates By Country

- U.S. format: MM/DD/YYYY (April 20, 2023)
- Canadian format: DD/MM/YYYY (20 April, 2023)

### Month And Year Date Ranges

- U.S. format, numerical: 4/1/2023 - 6/20/2023
- Months in the same year: Apr 4 - June 10, 2023
- Months in different years: Apr 2019 - June 2020

## Email

### Sample Email Address

If you need to include a sample email to show proper formatting for a user (for example, in error messages), use a human-centered example like `youremail@email.com`.

### Email Considerations

The organization automatically sends account- and system-related emails to confirm an action has been taken or to notify someone of an action they need to take. Account-related email communications should be clear, concise and informative.

Do not simply convert letter copy into an email. If the topic is too difficult to communicate in a couple of paragraphs, email may not be the best channel to use.

### Secure Messages

There is an alert email sent to customers letting them know they have received a secure message from their team. The actual message is not in the body of the email. It is only viewable in the Message section of customer portal once the customer has logged in.

### Email Disclosures

There are different disclosures depending on where the email originates (for example, marketing email vs. customer portal) and who is receiving it (customer or prospective customer). Check with Legal to determine the appropriate disclosure to use.

### Privacy Policy Links In Footers/Emails

Use the privacy link to drive to the Privacy and Security Center, rather than directly to the privacy policy, because it ties multiple privacy-related references together.

## Other Style Conventions

### Referring To A Screen, Button Or Section Of A Site

Use title case and bold letters when referring to a screen or button. Example: Go to the **Appointments Tab** for details about an upcoming appointment.

Select the **Submit** button to submit your request.

### Using Reference Numbers

Show real names over associate reference numbers in apps. If for some reason the reference is needed, use the name followed by P#. Example: Tony Faw (p134422).

### Zero / Empty State

Zero-state messages can be helpful in providing users with brief instructions on how to get started.

- Use `---` when the data is missing. This means, when data is available, it will be replaced with the value.
- Use an empty field to communicate if the field does not have and will never have a value.
- Display zeros (`0`) if you know what the value is and it just happens to be zero.

### Negative Numbers

Negative numbers should be dark text in light mode and should have a minus sign in front of the dollar sign with no space in between. Example: `-$10,000`.

### Displaying A customer's Account Number

Internal screens: Account numbers are displayed without dashes and followed by check digits. Eliminating the dashes in the account number allows the office to double-click on the account number for quick copy and paste. Format: `99712345 (1-1) (2-9) (4-5)`.

customer-facing screens: Account numbers should always be masked with asterisks (`****`) and only the last four digits should display. Example: `*****1234`.

Note: This differs from marketing content and letters that use `XXXX` to mask account numbers.

### Linking From customer Names Or customer Accounts

- When linking from a customer name, the link should always go to Activities & Notes. When linking from a customer's account number, the link should always go to their Holdings.
- The link text should display the name the account was given by the advisor using the Alpha Name field. Otherwise, display the registered account name.
- When only the customer's name is available (no account information), link text should be contextual to indicate where the user is going. Example: John Smith (Holdings).

### Log In

We prefer log in versus sign in. Use "log in," "sign up," and "log out" in verb form and "login," "signup" and "logout" as nouns and adjectives.

### Web Vs. App

When differentiating between web browsers and our mobile apps, we use the terms "web" and "app."

### Disclosures

- Disclosures specific to certain content should be displayed on the page where that content is located.
- Other disclosures specific to certain content are typically displayed as a callout above the content or at the bottom of that screen.
- If a disclosure applies to sub-sections, the disclosure is displayed on the page navigated from.
- Sometimes we link to terms or disclosures. For example, for customer portal, we link to disclosures posted on internal-domain.example.
- Legal and compliance will provide guidance if content requires different disclosure placement.
